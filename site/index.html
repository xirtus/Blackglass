BLACKGLASS // HYDRA

A satirical asymmetric information-war strategy game presented through two interfaces. Play BLACKGLASS, the surveillance-state containment system, or HYDRA, the leaker/truth network trying to make credible information impossible to suppress. Both sides inhabit the same synthetic world and social graph.

Real enemy: replication

Game in one sentence

Choose a side in the same deterministic information crisis: BLACKGLASS infers and contains from uncertain surveillance; HYDRA builds trusted, credible, independent disclosure branches before containment closes around the network.

DETECTA suspicious event or missing copy appears.
IDENTIFYDetermine who, what device and which copy matter.

INTERVENESpend scarce authority/resources to change the situation.
VERIFYDid the intervention destroy the branch or merely hide it?
CLEAN UPTrack descendants, publicity and new secondary problems.

Two playable perspectives

BLACKGLASS // Containment

Observe, infer, predict and intervene. You possess enormous surveillance reach but finite attention and no direct access to ground truth.

HYDRA // Disclosure

Trust, replicate, authenticate and release. You know your own branch but only imperfectly perceive state pressure and distant-network risk. Full second-campaign design →

What makes it fun

Inference under overload

The system can observe enormous quantities of synthetic data, but the player cannot inspect everything. False positives, missing nodes and conflicting models turn surveillance into detective work.

Hydra escalation

Every copied file can create descendants. Physical containment works early; later the player fights graphs, mirrors, public interest and distributed knowledge.

Power with consequences

Interventions are not “win buttons.” They exchange time for exposure, suspicion, collateral disruption, authority, legitimacy and the Streisand effect.

Production philosophy

Where visible game content should come from

Rule: do not hand-author ordinary reality. Generate roads, generic buildings, vegetation, terrain, crowds, weather and repeated visual systems. Source well-licensed generic nouns. Spend custom effort on the surveillance OS, hero characters, mission-specific spaces and fictional classified technology.
