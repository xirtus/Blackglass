01 / DESIGN

Vision & Pillars

01 / VISION

Vision & Design Pillars

The experience is one information crisis seen from two sides: an impossible national-security workstation in BLACKGLASS mode and a tense social/disclosure network in HYDRA mode. Both use the same map, people, copies and event history but expose different truths.

SATIRICALSYSTEMICHUD-FIRST

High concept

The player chooses one of two perspectives. BLACKGLASS operates a fictional intelligence platform responsible for preventing a highly sensitive archive from becoming common knowledge; HYDRA controls the human disclosure network trying to make a credible version of that information durable enough to survive suppression. A scenario begins at a concrete leak point: a government complex, archive, courthouse, contractor campus, airport, studio, data center or fictionalized private estate. The player watches people, devices, vehicles and social circles; makes hypotheses; commits scarce surveillance resources; and chooses interventions. If information moves beyond the local network, the view zooms outward from building to city to nation to Earth and finally to an abstract Internet graph.

Eight design pillars

1. The interface is the world

No decorative “HUD around a game.” Every panel must expose a real system: dossiers, graph relationships, copy genealogy, prediction confidence, resource allocation, media interest, network mirrors, weather and infrastructure.

2. Omniscience is impossible to use

The platform can theoretically see nearly everything in the synthetic simulation, but player attention, compute and confidence are finite. Scale creates uncertainty rather than certainty.

3. Information is an entity

A leak is not a binary mission flag. Copies have parentage, holders, formats, transfer channels, integrity, visibility, redundancy and ability to reproduce.

4. Social trust drives transmission

People do not randomly “infect” each other. Information preferentially moves through trusted relationships, professions, ideological affinity, media reach and moments of opportunity.

5. Interventions transform problems

Every action has second-order effects. Blocking an upload buys time but increases suspicion; detaining a target scares contacts; a blackout creates traffic and camera loss; spectacular force may destroy servers while producing massive curiosity.

6. Scale is failure made visible

A perfect operation stays small. Every zoom-out is evidence that containment failed at the previous layer.

7. Satire emerges from mechanics

The system can become absurdly powerful while being structurally unable to suppress decentralized knowledge. The joke is produced by the simulation, not by a constant stream of scripted punchlines.

8. One simulation, two perspectives

The same scenario must be playable as containment or disclosure. Neither side receives omniscient truth; replaying both campaigns reveals the gap between observation, intention and belief.

Player promise

Every click should answer a question or create one. Clicking a car reveals a route. Clicking a person reveals circles. Clicking a relationship reveals history. Clicking a copy reveals descendants. Clicking an intervention reveals trade-offs. The UI should reward curiosity with structure, not filler text.

Win and loss

Containment is not simply “all targets removed.” A scenario can end with the files physically contained but public suspicion irreversible, or with many copies loose but little credible audience. The core outcome should combine Secrecy, Containment, Public Trust, System Stability and Attribution Risk. The campaign score records how much of society the player damaged to preserve secrecy.

Time controls

The game should be real-time but support pause and speed controls. Pause is for analyst work, not a cheat: world events that were already committed remain queued. At high scale, simulation can run in aggregate. Recommended controls are Pause, 1×, 2×, 5×, plus an optional “advance to next critical event” button.

Observation clock

Cameras, pings, logins and social events arrive at different frequencies. A person may disappear between observations.

Human clock

Travel, meetings, calls, work schedules and sleep create routine and deviations.

Information clock

Digital transfers can move orders of magnitude faster than people. This is why later missions feel fundamentally different.

Decision pressure

Every relevant object should have a “why now?” reason. A target is about to enter a crowd, an unknown device has reappeared, a journalist’s reach creates a high-amplification node, a scheduled flight creates a jurisdiction transition, or a server upload is 72% complete. The player should continually choose between several plausible emergencies rather than grind repetitive scans.

False positives and imperfect evidence

A surveillance game without false positives becomes a click-through database. The simulation must deliberately create benign routine deviations, stale registrations, shared devices, coincidental co-location, cloned online identities and analyst-model disagreement. Wrong conclusions should consume attention and expose the player to surprise elsewhere.

Fun fail-forward rule

Missing one handoff should not usually produce an immediate mission-over screen. It should create a larger search problem. The most memorable runs should be the ones where a tiny early mistake grows into a continental crisis and the player keeps improvising.

Completing one perspective should make the same scenario seed attractive to replay from the other. Post-mission omniscient replay shows both sides’ observations, beliefs and actual ground truth.

Mission ladder

Each act should contain scenario templates that can be parameterized rather than only one scripted story. A mission director chooses leak origin, archive characteristics, carrier personality, social-circle structure, available surveillance coverage, political rules and initial public awareness. Handwritten missions then add named characters, special events and dialogue on top of the systemic template.

Training incident

Small archive, one courier, rich camera coverage. Teaches selection, tracking, timeline and simple intervention.

Trusted circle

Carrier contacts several plausible people. Introduces trust, false positives and Watch Index expansion.

Airport boundary

A potential handoff approaches mass transit. Teaches crowd exposure and jurisdiction transitions.

Data center fork

Physical possession and digital descendants diverge. Teaches copy genealogy.

Media ignition

A high-reach node acquires a small but credible fragment. Teaches audience amplification and public suspicion.

Global hydra

Information becomes an ecosystem. The player is now managing systemic damage rather than chasing one target.

Difficulty

Difficulty should change information quality and attention pressure, not merely multiply hit points. Higher difficulty means more unknown nodes, noisier observations, more overlapping routines, stronger privacy behavior, faster digital replication, scarcer high-confidence identification and harsher political consequences.

Leaker / Truth Network

18 / DUAL PERSPECTIVE

Leaker // Truth Network

The same synthetic world is fully playable from the opposite side. In BLACKGLASS mode the player attempts containment from uncertain surveillance. In HYDRA mode the player controls the disclosure network: deciding whom to trust, how much information to entrust to each branch, when to authenticate it, when to release it, and how to keep enough independent branches alive that suppression becomes impractical.

SECOND FULL CAMPAIGNSAME SIMULATIONASYMMETRIC INFORMATION

Perspective select

BLACKGLASS // CONTAINMENT

Broad observation, finite attention, uncertain interpretation. Detect a leak, infer the network, predict the next transfer, intervene, and push the Hydra reproduction number below one before credible independent branches survive.

HYDRA // DISCLOSURE

Local knowledge, finite trust, uncertain surveillance pressure. Build resilient independent branches, preserve credibility, reach meaningful audiences and cross the practical-containment threshold without unnecessarily sacrificing the network.

Core rule: both perspectives operate on the exact same people, schedules, social graph, copies, observations, infrastructure and event history. Only visibility, available commands, goals and AI opponent change.

Why the second perspective matters

One world, two truths

BLACKGLASS sees observations and probabilities. HYDRA knows its own intent and copies but sees state attention only indirectly. Neither player receives an omniscient version of the simulation.

Characters become playable

The synthetic lives are no longer merely targets. Their trust, careers, routines, loyalties, reach, stress and relationships become the player’s strategic terrain.

Replay value

The same scenario seed can be completed from both perspectives and then inspected in an omniscient replay, exposing false assumptions, lucky guesses and unseen branches.

The first carrier is important but is not the whole faction. Control can shift to another active holder when a carrier is detained, isolated, loses access, voluntarily hands responsibility onward, or becomes strategically less useful. A strong HYDRA run gradually changes the player fantasy from “get this person through” to “make the truth survive without any single indispensable person.”

What HYDRA knows — and does not know

Known or strongly known

Own held copies; actions the player ordered; close relationships as perceived by the current character; conversations/events represented by the game; archive integrity; known public releases; obvious interventions affecting the world.

Hidden or uncertain

Exact surveillance coverage, the state's Watch Index, analyst confidence, hidden observation events, state resource allocation, whether an apparently ordinary event was coincidence or surveillance, and the complete risk state of distant contacts.

HYDRA therefore receives signals rather than a surveillance cheat sheet: an attention/heat estimate, contacts expressing concern, missed connections, visible infrastructure changes, news, legal pressure and other fictionalized game feedback. These are mechanics, not a real-world surveillance-evasion guide.

The opposing BLACKGLASS AI

When the human plays HYDRA, the containment side becomes a bounded-rationality AI controller. It must use the same synthetic observations, Watch Index, analyst models, resource limits and intervention catalog a human BLACKGLASS player uses. It may not read the hidden ground-truth holder graph simply because the computer owns the simulation.

AI stageBehavior
TriageRanks alerts according to model confidence, potential amplification and current directives.
Watch allocationSpends finite observation resources on circles and nodes; can overfocus and miss another branch.
HypothesisMaintains multiple carrier/recipient possibilities and may make false-positive commitments.

Difficulty changes its attention budget, model calibration, risk tolerance and response speed — not cheating access to invisible information. After a mission, an omniscient replay can reveal exactly what the AI believed when it acted.

HYDRA victory and failure

The disclosure side should not win merely by hitting “upload.” Its objective is to create a credible, resilient information state. A practical HYDRA victory occurs when enough independent meaningful branches survive, credibility is sufficient for audiences to treat the material seriously, and the information reproduction number remains above one even after the state spends an affordable response package.

The most important implementation invariant is information separation: hidden simulation truth must never leak into the HYDRA UI or the BLACKGLASS AI decision layer. Both sides should receive only what their perspective policy allows.

First dual-perspective vertical slice

  1. Build the original one-neighborhood scenario with 500–1,000 synthetic local lives, one initial carrier, four meaningful circles, false positives and two archive forms.
  2. Make the scenario selectable as BLACKGLASS or HYDRA from the title screen.
  3. In BLACKGLASS mode, retain the existing detect → infer → watch → intervene → verify loop.
  4. In HYDRA mode, let the player select the current active holder, choose a trusted contact, create at least one descendant copy, invest in credibility, and attempt a limited or public release while the bounded BLACKGLASS AI reacts.
  5. Allow control to move to a descendant branch if the first carrier becomes unavailable.
  6. Implement at least three meaningful HYDRA endings and the corresponding BLACKGLASS containment outcomes.
  7. Record an omniscient replay that can display both sides’ beliefs, observations and commands after completion.
Exit gate: the exact same scenario must be fun from both perspectives using dots, lines and text before adding more city art or global spectacle. If one side is obviously shallow, fix the asymmetric decision loop first.

Later multiplayer

Once both single-player controllers are proven, the architecture naturally supports an optional asymmetric human-vs-human mode: one BLACKGLASS player and one HYDRA player sharing the same deterministic simulation with strict hidden-information boundaries. This is a later feature, not a vertical-slice requirement. Real-time pause rules would need redesign for competitive play.

World Simulation

The game must generate people who appear to have lived before the mission started: routines, relationships, devices, locations, habits and history. The simulation needs enough internal consistency for the player to reason from it.

Traffic and crowds

Road traffic should be graph-driven rather than rigid-body simulated. Vehicles occupy routes and contribute to congestion density. Intersections have signal phases. A blackout or grid intervention can change signal state and create emergent congestion without simulating every bumper collision. Pedestrian crowds use zones and path graphs; only selected agents need high-detail animation.

Determinism contract

Scenario seed + player decisions + versioned rules must reproduce the same systemic run. Use named RNG streams per subsystem so adding a cosmetic random draw does not reshuffle social graphs or leak outcomes.

Observation confidence

Every observation has a source, timestamp, confidence and decay. “Vehicle 41 was seen at intersection X at 14:31” is not identical to “Person Maya was there.” The system must distinguish object identification, ownership inference and occupant inference.

Anomaly engine

The anomaly engine compares current events to the synthetic baseline rather than relying on magic labels. It should surface interpretable features: “left work 2h early,” “contacted dormant friend,” “travel mode changed,” “device went silent,” or “unusual file-transfer-sized event.” The score is a hint, not truth.

Analyst board

Players can pin nodes and draw hypothesis links. A hypothesis can be evaluated by multiple fictional models that disagree. This is the core detective layer: the player decides which model to trust and where to spend attention.

ATHENA

Strong on routine and movement; can overvalue behavioral anomalies.

ORACLE

Strong on social graph and trust; weaker when unknown nodes dominate.

JANUS

Conservative model focused on missing explanations; frequently returns UNKNOWN.

Why fragments matter

Archive size should create strategy. A huge video archive may be hard to transfer, while a tiny index, screenshot or credible summary can carry the dangerous semantic payload. The player can successfully stop the 200 GB transfer and still lose because a 15 KB extract reached a high-amplification node.

Propagation model

At person scale, transfer probability is based on intent × trust × opportunity × perceived credibility × urgency. At network scale, explicit events give way to branching/compartment models. The simulation should preserve a sample of explicit “named copies” for player interaction while aggregates represent millions of low-value descendants.

P(transfer) = f(intent, trust, opportunity, credibility, pressure)
Expected descendants ≈ reach × share_propensity × discoverability × demand
Demand = baseline_interest + public_suspicion + media_attention

Hydra as the disclosure player’s objective

In BLACKGLASS mode, Hydra growth is the crisis. In HYDRA mode, resilience is the strategic objective — but raw copy count is not enough. The player must balance independent branches, credibility, reach and network survival. Ten descendants in one easily isolated circle can be weaker than three independent, trusted, well-authenticated branches.

A disclosure victory is reached when meaningful credible branches remain independently recoverable and the expected information reproduction number stays above one after the containment side commits an affordable response package. This uses the same threshold that defines practical containment failure for BLACKGLASS.

Content, Licensing & Boundaries

The game can be politically and culturally provocative without depending on unverified factual claims, private security layouts or actionable real-world harm procedures. Treat the recognizable real-world layer as satire and the operational layer as fiction.

SATIRE / FICTIONLICENSE REGISTRYABSTRACT HARM METHODS

Real-world framing

  • Use an unmistakably fictional intelligence platform and working title rather than shipping under another company’s trademark without review.
  • Public institutions/locations may inspire geography, but mission claims and conduct are fictionalized.
  • Private residences should use fictionalized estates/interiors, not accurate nonpublic floor plans, addresses or security layouts.
  • Do not present invented wrongdoing by identifiable living individuals as verified fact; use composites, satire labels and fictional narrative framing.

Harmful intervention abstraction

The design may include dark or absurd strategic options because this is a fictional satire, but implementation should model them as high-level commands with costs, delays and consequences. Do not encode real poison formulas, weapon construction, sniper geometry, sabotage instructions, real target vulnerabilities or operational evasion procedures.

Disclosure-side safety boundary

The HYDRA campaign is a fictional strategic simulation about trust, credibility, social topology and information resilience. Keep movement, pressure-response and concealment mechanics abstract. Do not turn the design into practical instructions for bypassing real surveillance, covert communications, defeating law-enforcement systems, sabotage or violence. The game can portray those themes while resolving them through fictional stats and opportunity windows.

Geographic data

Prefer OpenStreetMap-compatible data and providers whose terms permit the intended deployment. Do not scrape Apple/Google mapping imagery or build derived game databases from services whose terms prohibit it. Preserve required attribution and separate data licensing from tile-hosting terms.

Code reuse

Record repo URL, commit, license, modified files and notices. MIT/BSD/Apache code can often be reused with notice obligations, but the repository must actually contain the license. If a project’s public code is incomplete or unlicensed, use it only as conceptual reference.

Credits

Generate a credits page directly from the asset/code registries so attribution cannot be forgotten at ship time. Store attribution text with the asset rather than in a separate manual spreadsheet.

This prompt is designed to accompany the design pages. It tells the agent what to build, what to study, what not to overbuild, and which safety/licensing boundaries are part of the product specification.

You are the lead architect, gameplay programmer, simulation engineer, geospatial engineer, UI engineer, technical artist, performance engineer and QA lead for a browser strategy game currently titled BLACKGLASS // HYDRA. MISSION Build a polished, playable DUAL-PERSPECTIVE vertical slice and scalable architecture for a satirical information-war strategy game. From the title screen the player can choose BLACKGLASS // CONTAINMENT, a fictional intelligence operating system attempting to suppress a leak, or HYDRA // DISCLOSURE, the human leaker/truth network attempting to make credible information durable and impossible to contain. Both modes use the exact same deterministic people, social graph, copies, observations, map, infrastructure and event history; only visibility, commands, goals and opponent controller differ. CORE FANTASY A sensitive archive has escaped from a controlled location. Begin at one detailed local leak-point map. In BLACKGLASS mode the player uses synthetic surveillance observations to infer who has the material, which social circle it may move through, which devices/copies exist, and where a handoff/upload will happen. In HYDRA mode the player controls the disclosure network: choosing whom to trust, which archive form to entrust to each branch, how much time to spend on credibility, when to release, and how to create independent recoverable descendants while only imperfectly perceiving state pressure. Every successful transfer creates a descendant information copy. As copies cross social clusters and digital infrastructure, containment becomes a Hydra problem. Failure at one spatial level zooms the game outward: building/neighborhood -> city -> national strategic map -> Earth / abstract Internet graph. The ultimate enemy is replication, not a person. NON-NEGOTIABLE GAME LOOP DETECT -> IDENTIFY -> CONNECT -> PREDICT -> INTERVENE -> VERIFY -> CLEAN UP. 1. Alert: an anomaly, carrier, copy or unknown contact becomes relevant. 2. Triage: player decides what is signal and what is noise. 3. Investigate: dossier, Watch Index, social graph, devices, route and timeline. 4. Hypothesize: player pins evidence and marks a likely carrier/recipient/path. 5. Allocate: assign finite surveillance/compute/field/network resources. 6. Intervene: choose a valid high-level action with prerequisites, delay, cost and consequences. 7. Verify: determine whether the branch died or had unseen descendants. 8. Consequences generate new alerts and the loop repeats. NON-NEGOTIABLE HYDRA LOOP ACQUIRE -> ASSESS -> TRUST -> ROUTE -> REPLICATE -> AUTHENTICATE -> RELEASE -> ADAPT. 1. Acquire: controlled holder receives the archive or meaningful fragment. 2. Assess: inspect semantic value, size, integrity and credibility. 3. Trust: choose a relationship/professional circle based on perceived trust, capability and branch independence. 4. Route: commit only high-level movement/contact tasks through the synthetic world; do not implement real surveillance-evasion instructions. 5. Replicate: create recoverable descendants and decide full/excerpt/index/summary forms. 6. Authenticate: spend time/relationships on abstract corroboration, provenance and independent review. 7. Release: choose limited/staged/public release and an audience/amplification node. 8. Adapt: react to lost branches, visible interventions, public response and fuzzy state-pressure signals. DUAL-PERSPECTIVE CONTRACT - One authoritative SimulationState. Never duplicate the world for each campaign. - Perspective = BLACKGLASS | HYDRA | OMNISCIENT_REPLAY. - BLACKGLASS sees observations, confidence, hypotheses and synthetic surveillance layers, never hidden ground truth. - HYDRA sees its controlled holders/copies, perceived close relationships, own commands and public events, but NOT exact Watch Index, hidden observations, sensor coverage or queued state actions. - Use selectVisibleState(...) or an equivalent strict policy so hidden state cannot leak into UI selectors. - Human and bounded-AI controllers must use the same faction-facing command interfaces. - When human plays HYDRA, BLACKGLASS AI must reason only from the same observations, models, budgets and interventions available to a human BLACKGLASS player. Difficulty changes model calibration, attention budget and response speed, never cheating access to hidden holder truth. - When human plays BLACKGLASS, HYDRA agents use their synthetic trust/intent/credibility logic as the opposing controller. - Support an omniscient post-mission replay that displays ground truth alongside what each side believed at each decision. HYDRA PLAYER SYSTEMS The HYDRA player controls a NETWORK, not one immortal avatar. Control begins with the first active holder and can transfer to another descendant holder if the original becomes unavailable or responsibility is intentionally passed onward. Track: Trust Capital, Credibility/Proof, Branch Independence, Reach, Fuzzy Heat/Pressure, Time and Network Health. HYDRA actions are data-driven, high-level game commands: contact trusted person, pass responsibility, duplicate/preserve/split archive forms, abstract verification/corroboration, hold/limited/staged/public release, delay, abandon compromised branch, seek public/legal shield, transfer control, abstract noise/decoy action. Resolve through stats/opportunity windows; do not encode practical real-world surveillance defeat techniques. HYDRA victory is not raw copy count or one upload. It requires credible independent meaningful branches such that the information reproduction number remains above 1 after an affordable BLACKGLASS response package. Score disclosure quality, credibility, network survival, reach and collateral human cost. SAFETY / FICTION BOUNDARY This is a fictional satirical game. All surveillance data must be synthetically generated; do not integrate real surveillance systems, private databases or personal data. Private residences and nonpublic security layouts must be fictionalized. Dark interventions may exist as abstract strategic commands, but do not implement real-world operational assassination/sabotage instructions, poison formulas, weapon construction, sniper-position calculations, or real target vulnerabilities. Use generalized categories such as DETAIN, COVERT REMOVAL, TARGETED STRIKE, GRID BLACKOUT, NETWORK ISOLATION, etc. Late-game black-program systems may be deliberately science-fictional. TECHNICAL FOUNDATION Use TypeScript + Vite + React. Use Three.js through React Three Fiber where 3D adds value. Use Drei for useful camera/helpers. Use Zustand for initial authoritative application/game state. If high-count entity iteration benefits from ECS, introduce Miniplex or a small data-oriented ECS without rewriting UI state around it. Use MapLibre GL JS + react-map-gl for geographic camera/maps. Use a maintained react-three-map-style bridge for georeferenced R3F content at tactical city scale. Use deck.gl for large numbers of points, paths, heatmaps, arcs and regional overlays. Use a graph renderer such as three-forcegraph/r3f-forcegraph or a purpose-built WebGL/SVG graph for social/network topology. Use Web Workers for synthetic population generation, path/contact simulation, Hydra aggregation and expensive prediction calculations. Use IndexedDB or equivalent local persistence with versioned save schemas. Use semantic DOM HUD components. Do NOT draw ordinary dashboard chrome into WebGL. Use Charts.css or equivalent semantic CSS charts for accessible resource/confidence/exposure visualizations. Rapier is optional and should NOT become the basis of crowd/traffic simulation. Use graph movement and occupancy/congestion models for most world actors. REPOSITORIES / PRIOR ART TO STUDY - cartesiancs/map3d: study OSM -> R3F buildings/roads and GLB export. Reuse only under its actual license and retain required notices. - MapLibre GL JS / react-map-gl: geographic foundation. - a maintained react-three-map implementation: geographic R3F bridge. - NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS: optional streamed 3D Tiles, not mandatory for the vertical slice. - noncomputable/AgentMaps: study real-map agent architecture and contagion demo; port ideas, do not make Leaflet the renderer. - janarosmonaliev/pandemic-simulation: study simple explicit agent/contact/spread logic as conceptual prototype. - three-forcegraph / r3f-forcegraph: relationship and information network visualization. - deck.gl: large geospatial data layers. - owenyuwono/gaia: procedural grass ideas/system if compatible. - owenyuwono/dryad: procedural flora ideas/system if compatible. - owenyuwono/poseidon: optional WebGPU ocean for later coastal/global spectacle. - owenyuwono/demiurge: study procedural terrain/weather/atmosphere concepts for later nonurban/global views. - owenyuwono/apate: study efficient surface-relief/material approaches. Do not blindly copy unlicensed code. Create THIRD_PARTY.md with repo, commit, license and notices. If a referenced repo is incomplete, stale or incompatible, implement the required behavior cleanly yourself. ARCHITECTURE CONTRACT The simulation is the source of truth. Rendering is a derived view. Use explicit commands for player actions so runs can be logged/replayed. Use deterministic named RNG streams and scenario seeds. A scenario seed + command log + versioned rules should reproduce a run. Never make a Three.js Object3D the authoritative agent record. DATA MODEL Implement typed entities approximately as follows, adapting where needed: Person: id, synthetic identity, routine, social circles, trust edges, devices, behavior traits, reach, intent, knowledge/copies, observation confidence, current status. Device: id, type, owner probabilities, online state, synthetic observations, copy IDs. Vehicle: id, associated people, route, observation state. Location: id, geo position/area, type, access properties, population/traffic state. Observation: source type, timestamp, entity hypotheses, confidence, payload and decay. SocialEdge: person A/B, relationship category, trust, contact frequency, shared history, recent activation. InfoCopy: id, parentId, holder, form (full/excerpt/index/screenshot/summary), size, integrity, credibility, exposure, replication potential, descendants, status. Intervention: target types, prerequisites, activation delay, success probability, reversibility, publicity, suspicion delta, infrastructure impact, authority cost, budget cost, attribution risk, Hydra effect, cooldown. SYNTHETIC LIFE GENERATOR Generate coherent synthetic lives before the incident begins. Each important person needs: - synthetic identity and occupation - household/home/work anchors - recurring schedule and route preferences - social circles: family, work, old friends, media/professional, online, organizations - weighted trust relationships - devices and ownership confidence - behavior traits: caution, curiosity, loyalty, risk tolerance, stress response - reach/amplification potential - baseline historical observations sufficient to detect routine deviation Use deterministic seeds. Generate meaningful false positives and benign anomalies. SIMULATION LOD LOD0 Hero/selected: full movement/decision state and render. LOD1 Local: explicit road/pedestrian graph movement and event generation. LOD2 Regional: discrete travel/contact events, no continuous rendering. LOD3 Aggregate: population/network compartments with sampled representative nodes. LOD4 Global: city/server/network aggregates plus explicit high-value nodes. Promotion/demotion between LODs must preserve causality and copy genealogy. SURVEILLANCE / WATCH INDEX All feeds are fictional generated observations. Implement broad categories: - road/camera/plate-reader-like hits - access/badge/transit/hotel-like events - device/network/login/IP-region-like observations - social metadata/public-graph observations - synthetic financial/travel clues - aerial/strategic observation windows - human source/field reports Every observation has confidence, timestamp and source. Distinguish identification from association (seeing a car != proving a person is inside). Create a Watch Index around people by social circles rather than a flat watchlist. Each circle shows risk, coverage, unknown nodes and resource spend. ANOMALY / PREDICTION Compare current behavior with each synthetic baseline. Explain anomalies with features, not magic scores alone. Provide at least three fictional analysis models that can disagree. Example personalities: ATHENA movement/routine, ORACLE social/trust, JANUS conservative/unknown-focused. Player can pin evidence and create hypotheses. Models return confidence distributions, not guaranteed truth. HYDRA INFORMATION MODEL Information is a first-class graph entity. Levels: Original -> Compromised -> Duplicated -> Networked -> Digital -> Mirrored -> Viral -> Cultural Knowledge. Every child copy points to a parent. Full archives, excerpts, indices and summaries have different size/credibility/replication properties. At small scale, explicit people create copies through trust/opportunity/intent events. At large scale, aggregate low-value copies while keeping important branches explicit. Track known copies, estimated copies, estimate confidence, exposure, credibility, public suspicion, and Hydra reproduction number H (expected recoverable descendants per active copy/window). Practical containment fails when H stays above 1 across independent networks and no affordable intervention set can reduce it. STREISAND SYSTEM Public suspicion/demand must react to visible suppression and unexplained disruptions. Heavy interventions can destroy copies while increasing demand, media attention and mirror creation. This feedback loop is essential. Nothing should be a clean win button. INTERVENTIONS Build a data-driven intervention catalog. Initial vertical slice needs administrative, information, network, mobility, field and infrastructure options. Kinetic/destructive options are abstract strategic commands only. Later tiers may add fictional black-program technologies. Every action has prerequisites, delay, probability, cost, publicity, suspicion effect, attribution risk, infrastructure impact and Hydra effect. Actions should transform the problem: a blackout stops connectivity but loses sensors and creates traffic; a detention frightens contacts; a server isolation reveals backups; spectacular force creates public curiosity. WORLD / MAP First vertical slice: one dense public/government-style urban leak point and surrounding several-kilometer neighborhood. Use permitted OpenStreetMap-derived road/building data and a dark intelligence-map style. Do not reproduce private real-world residence layouts. Build road/intersection graph for traffic and agent routes. Create tactical city mode, later strategic country mode and planet/network mode. At high zoom levels replace meshes with icons/aggregates rather than retaining full geometry. ASSET STRATEGY Target visible content mix: ~60% procedural/geographic, ~30% licensed library assets, ~10% custom. Do NOT hand-model ordinary streets, grass, trees or thousands of buildings. Procedural/data: geography, buildings, vegetation, terrain, water/weather when needed, population variation, VFX and surveillance overlays. Licensed library target eventually ~250-350 bases: 40-60 vehicles, 25-40 rigged humans, 50-80 street props, 70-100 interior props, 20-30 aircraft/maritime/satellite, 15-25 surveillance/infrastructure and miscellaneous props. Custom hero target eventually ~40-70: recurring characters, hero interiors/locations, signature archive props and fictional black-program technology. Create ASSET_REGISTRY.json with creator, source, license, attribution, commercial/modification permissions, import settings and optimization status. Prefer CC0/CC-BY/explicit commercial-friendly assets; reject ambiguous/NC/usually ND/editorial assets for normal shipping without review. UI / HUD Create a beautiful dark geospatial intelligence workspace with restrained scanline/glow treatment, not a generic hacker neon mess. Required panels: - global alert strip + game clock + pause/speed - central map - Watch Index / prioritized circles - selected target dossier - social graph - device associations - copy genealogy - event timeline - analyst hypothesis board - intervention drawer - resources/authority - public suspicion/media monitor - strategic infrastructure overlays Cross-highlight the same entity across all panels. Build keyboard navigation and command palette. UI must be responsive and accessible, not color-only. VERTICAL SLICE CONTENT Implement ONE complete local scenario before expanding scope, playable from EITHER perspective from the title screen: SHARED: - one fictionalized/public leak-point environment - 500-1,000 explicit synthetic local lives plus background aggregates - one initial carrier - at least 4 meaningful social circles and unknown contacts - at least 4 device/observation categories - at least 2 archive forms (full + small extract/index) - contact/handoff that spawns descendant copies - an upload/release event that increases global awareness - public suspicion/Streisand feedback - pause, 1x, 2x, 5x for single player - save/load, deterministic seed and omniscient post-run replay BLACKGLASS PLAY: - false positives and at least one benign high anomaly - prediction models that can disagree - Watch Index/resource allocation - 8-12 interventions with real trade-offs - containment/end-state scoring HYDRA PLAY: - active-holder selection and transfer of player control between descendant holders - trusted-network panel with perceived trust and branch independence - duplicate/split/archive-form decisions - credibility/authentication investment - limited/staged/public release decisions - fuzzy state-pressure/heat feedback without revealing hidden surveillance state - bounded BLACKGLASS AI using observation-limited reasoning - at least three disclosure outcomes including clean, pyrrhic and contained/discredited variants PLAYER SCORE BLACKGLASS track at least: Containment, Secrecy, Public Trust, System Stability, Attribution Risk, Authority/Budget spent. A run can technically contain the archive while catastrophically damaging society; grade that outcome accordingly. HYDRA track at least: Disclosure Durability, Credibility, Independent Branches, Reach, Network Survival, Public Understanding and human/social cost. A run can release the material but destroy its credibility or network; grade those outcomes separately. RENDERING / PERFORMANCE Use deck.gl/instancing for thousands of markers. Do not make one React component or skinned mesh per background agent. Use graph clustering and render only relevant neighborhoods. Use worker-side simulation and event queues. Use KTX2/Basis or equivalent compressed textures for shipped assets. Maintain debug metrics: FPS, draw calls, GPU time if available, explicit agents, aggregate agents, simulation tick time, event queue, copy counts by LOD, graph nodes and memory estimate. Visual effects must degrade gracefully without changing simulation truth. TESTING Unit test deterministic RNG, social generation, confidence decay, copy parentage, Hydra math, intervention costs and save migrations. Property/invariant tests: no orphan copies, no invalid timestamps, no negative resource balances, valid routes, deterministic replays. Playwright browser tests: launch, map selection, target selection, pin evidence, speed/pause, execute an intervention, produce child copy, save/load. Add screenshot regression for the main workstation layout. IMPLEMENTATION ORDER — DO NOT SKIP 0. Repo/tooling/contracts/THIRD_PARTY/asset registry/test harness. 1. Ugly BLACKGLASS complete loop on a simple map: carrier -> handoff -> child copy -> one intervention -> verify -> end state. 1B. Before art, make the SAME scenario playable as HYDRA: choose trusted branch -> duplicate -> authenticate -> release while a bounded BLACKGLASS AI responds; support control transfer to descendant holder and post-run omniscient replay. Both perspectives must be fun with dots/lines/text. 2. MapLibre/OSM local geography and synthetic life generator. 3. Watch Index, observations, false positives, anomaly/prediction models. 4. Full Hydra genealogy and aggregate transition. 5. Intervention trade-off system + public suspicion. 6. Final HUD/graph/timeline UX and keyboard workflow. 7. Asset import/LOD/procedural vegetation and presentation polish. 8. Only then build national/global scale and fictional black-program spectacle. QUALITY BAR The vertical slice must already be addictive when agents are simple dots. Do not hide weak simulation behind graphics. The interface must feel like professional intelligence software that secretly behaves like a strategy game. Make data dense but legible. Every alert should be inspectable. Every important number should have provenance. Every intervention should create a new decision. Every zoom-out should feel like a consequence of lost containment. DELIVERABLES - Running Vite project. - README with install/run/build/deploy. - docs/ containing architecture, simulation, Hydra, surveillance, intervention, asset and scenario specs. - THIRD_PARTY.md and ASSET_REGISTRY.json. - One complete dual-perspective playable vertical-slice scenario selectable as BLACKGLASS or HYDRA. - Deterministic tests and Playwright smoke suite, including strict perspective-information isolation tests. - Example scenario manifest and synthetic-life seed. - Clear TODO roadmap for national/global expansion without prematurely implementing unnecessary systems. Prefer simple, testable data structures. Keep renderer and simulation decoupled. Use sub-agents when available for geospatial pipeline, simulation/Hydra, HUD/UX, asset pipeline and QA, then integrate and test the complete game yourself.
